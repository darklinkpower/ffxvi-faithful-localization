**Please read first:** Large language models (LLMs) were used as a support tool to produce the initial translation drafts directly from the Japanese script. I then carried out an extensive editing and review process, checking accuracy, meaning, nuance, characterization, tone, and consistency, while refining the English wording and style. I have personally reviewed, edited, and refined the translation. Nevertheless, if you are not comfortable with AI-assisted translation, please consider skipping this mod.

## Description

A complete retranslation of the game's script, made directly from the original Japanese and independent of the official English localization. It aims to present the dialogue as faithfully as possible, preserving meaning, tone, ambiguity, pacing, and characterization without reinterpretation, editorialization, or modernization.

It is not an edit, tweak, or revision of the official English script. Every line was translated from the Japanese.

The current release covers the base game's script. I don't currently own the DLC, so it is not included. I may add it in the future if I'm able to acquire it.

**Scope and compatibility:** This project covers the game's main script only. UI text and other non-script content, including menus and item descriptions, retain the original localization and terminology. This is intentional: keeping those terms unchanged helps maintain consistency between players using this retranslation and those using the official English script, so both can still refer to the same names and terms without confusion.

**Who this mod is for:** This project is intended for players who want to experience the story closer to its original Japanese presentation. It may be particularly appealing to those playing with Japanese audio who want an English script that reflects the dialogue they hear, making it easier to follow along while retaining the original meaning and nuances.

**A note on the official localization:** This project does not claim that the official localization is "wrong." It recognizes that localization sometimes involves reinterpretation and adaptation beyond a direct translation. Many players will enjoy the official version as it is, and nothing here is meant to take that away.

## Why this mod exists

The official English script is a localization, but in the passages I compared, it often goes beyond adapting the Japanese into natural English and rewrites the dialogue itself. These differences were not limited to isolated wording choices. They repeatedly affected meaning, characterization, tone, and the way scenes unfold. Correcting individual lines did not seem sufficient; the more consistent approach was to return to the Japanese source and retranslate the script as a whole.

These are the recurring patterns I found when comparing the Japanese with the official English:

**Rewriting instead of translating.** Dialogue is sometimes replaced with newly written material that introduces different motivations, intentions, explanations, or reactions. In some cases, entire exchanges are reshaped, changing what characters communicate and how they respond to one another.

**Loss of ambiguity and restraint.** The Japanese often relies on implication, ellipsis, silence, understatement, and open-ended phrasing. The English sometimes fills these gaps with added explanations, emotional cues, or details that are not present in the source. It can also turn an implication into an explicit statement or settle something the original leaves ambiguous. While these additions may make the dialogue more immediately expressive, they can narrow the range of possible interpretations and introduce ideas or nuances that the Japanese does not establish.

**Characterization and voice drift.** Changes in wording and delivery can make characters sound more sarcastic, confrontational, witty, sentimental, or expressive than they do in Japanese. Distinctive speech patterns and restrained personalities can be replaced by a more uniformly quippy or theatrical style. This affects not only individual lines but also the impression characters leave over the course of the story: their temperament, relationships, and the way they express themselves. A character's voice is part of their characterization, and changing it can change how they are perceived.

**Added moral framing and commentary.** Some lines introduce reassurance, condemnation, approval, judgment, or commentary that the Japanese does not express, either explicitly or implicitly. Rather than simply presenting a situation or allowing a character's words to speak for themselves, the English can add an explicit interpretation or steer the player toward a particular reaction.

**Emotional inflation and altered rhythm.** Short, understated, or hesitant lines are sometimes expanded into more emphatic dialogue, with added exclamations, flourishes, jokes, and heightened emotional delivery. This changes the rhythm of conversations: pauses and fragments become fuller statements, quiet moments become more dramatic, and concise exchanges lose some of their original impact. The result can feel more verbose and performative than the source.

**Softening and reframing.** Harsh, uncomfortable, or explicit implications are sometimes euphemized or replaced with less direct language. In other cases, the underlying subject is changed altogether. This can obscure what a character actually says, alter the severity of their words, change backstories, or shift the emotional and thematic weight of a scene.

**A different overall dialogue style.** The official English often favors a more embellished, quip-heavy register, turning straightforward observations and simple responses into witty remarks, elaborate flourishes, or lines with an added sense of humor. This is not necessarily inappropriate in isolation, but when applied repeatedly, it gives conversations a different character from the Japanese. Even minor exchanges can acquire a playful or theatrical quality that was not present in the original.

Taken together, these patterns affect more than translation accuracy. They change the texture of the dialogue: how characters sound, how they relate to one another, how scenes build and release tension, and how much the script leaves to the player's interpretation. This project aims to preserve those qualities by translating what the Japanese actually says, including its restraint, ambiguity, rhythm, and individual character voices, rather than replacing them with a different style of dialogue.

## Translation approach

The retranslation follows a source-text-first approach, with the aim of preserving the original dialogue without unnecessary additions, omissions, or changes:

- Translate what is written, without introducing material or implications that are not in the source.
- Preserve ambiguity instead of resolving it, and leave what is unsaid unspoken.
- Avoid adding, removing, or altering emotional emphasis, judgment, intent, or explanation.
- Match brevity with brevity and silence with silence. A short line stays short, and a vague line stays vague.
- Preserve each character's distinct voice, manner of speaking, and way of addressing others, rather than imposing a uniform dialogue style.

Particular care was taken with characterization and voice consistency. Each character's speech patterns and manner of expression were considered not only individually, but also in relation to other characters and the circumstances of each scene. Scene-specific analyses were prepared to guide how characters speak, how they interact with one another, and how their voices should come across in different situations.

The goal is to preserve the original script's meaning, tone, characterization, and rhythm as closely as possible. This is a preservation effort.
